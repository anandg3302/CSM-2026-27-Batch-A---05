## 1. Executive Summary

In MediaTrace Stage A.2.3, platform engineering expanded the controlled validation of the two previously verified facial video deepfake detectors:
1. **VideoMAE** (`Vansh180/VideoMae-ffc23-deepfake-detector` @ commit `ea26518b527d938d66e568c52bcf1c19a86cf295`)
2. **RealForensics** (`ahaliassos/RealForensics` @ commit `a53e849407d2fd5be0f0a7eb3df7d1a846b5b803`)

The validation cohort was expanded from the 7 samples evaluated in Stage A.1 and A.2.2 to **26 samples**:
* **10 human facial videos** (`v1.mp4` through `v10.mp4`) sourced from Pexels via the independently documented SDFVD (Small-scale Deepfake Forgery Video Dataset);
* **10 manipulated facial videos** (`vs1.mp4` through `vs10.mp4`) generated by Remaker AI facial swapping from the respective human source videos;
* **6 transformation derivatives** (3 human derivatives: `v1_resize50.mp4`, `v1_resize75.mp4`, `v2_resize50.mp4`; 3 manipulated derivatives: `vs1_resize50.mp4`, `vs1_resize75.mp4`, `vs2_resize50.mp4`).

### Key Empirical Findings

* **Signal Diversity:** RealForensics exhibited a different output pattern from VideoMAE on the controlled validation samples, including different classifications on several known-human and known-manipulated videos. This demonstrates signal diversity in this experiment but does not establish detector reliability or forensic validity.
* **Comparative Agreement Matrix (20 Primary Samples):**
  * `both_agree_with_known_category`: **7 / 20** (35.0%)
  * `both_disagree_with_known_category`: **6 / 20** (30.0%)
  * `models_disagree`: **7 / 20** (35.0%)
* **Transformation Robustness (6 Derivatives):** Both detectors demonstrated 100% (6/6) class preservation across spatial downscaling (50% and 75% downscaling), confirming stability to basic spatial resizing.
* **Strict Validation Boundary:** Zero changes were made to production services, API endpoints, the forensic decision framework, or frontend UI.

## 2. Validation Methodology & Hard Scope Boundaries

This stage was conducted under strict forensic validation rules:

1. **Validation-Only Scope:** Neither detector's production implementation was altered. No new production routes were added. RealForensics remains unintegrated into `/api/analysis/video/{case_id}`.
2. **No Score Fusion or Unified Probability:** Detector outputs are fundamentally different mathematical objects:
   * VideoMAE outputs an uncalibrated probability of the fake class ($p \in [0, 1]$) derived from softmax over two logits;
   * RealForensics outputs an uncalibrated unbounded scalar logit ($z \in \mathbb{R}$).
   They were **not** averaged, weighted, or fused into a combined score.
3. **No Threshold Tuning:** Decision boundaries were evaluated strictly according to official training semantics:
   * VideoMAE: argmax class selection (`fake` if $p > 0.5$, else `real`);
   * RealForensics: scalar logit sign (`manipulated` if $z > 0$, else `human`).
   No thresholds were adjusted to fit the SDFVD validation cohort.
4. **Descriptive Empirical Characterization:** All statistics are reported as descriptive characterizations of this specific out-of-domain sample cohort, not generalizable accuracy claims.

## 5. Experimental Protocol & Preprocessing Pipelines

### VideoMAE Processing Pipeline
* **Frame Selection:** 16 uniformly sampled frames spanning the entire video duration.
* **Facial Gating:** YCbCr skin chrominance and Haar cascade face verification.
* **Spatial Preprocessing:** Aspect-preserving resize of shortest edge to 224, center crop to 224x224, ImageNet normalization.
* **Input Tensor:** $(1, 16, 3, 224, 224)$ float32.
* **Inference:** Pure NumPy tubelet patch embedding ($1568$ tokens of dim $768$), 12 transformer encoder layers, classification head.

### RealForensics Processing Pipeline
* **Frame Selection:** Contiguous block of 25 consecutive frames ($T=25$, duration $\sim 0.83$s) centered at the video midpoint.
* **Landmark Detection:** 68 2D landmarks per frame detected using 2D-FAN.
* **Temporal Smoothing:** Window margin $w=12$ frames.
* **Affine Alignment:** Partial 2D affine warp using 8 stable facial points onto `20words_mean_face.npy`.
* **Patch Cropping & Normalization:** $150 \times 150$ patch centered on lower face/mouth, center crop to $140 \times 140$, resize to $112 \times 112$, ImageNet normalization.
* **Input Tensor:** $(1, 3, 25, 112, 112)$ float32.
* **Inference:** PyTorch 3D CSN-101 with MeanLinear head ($scale=64.0$).

## 7. VideoMAE Individual Model Evaluation (Descriptive)

* **Known Human Samples ($n=10$):**
  * VideoMAE selected the `fake` class for all 10 known-human SDFVD samples in this expanded cohort.
  * Predicted fake probabilities ranged from `0.5224` (`v4.mp4`) to `0.9663` (`v8.mp4`), with a mean fake score of `0.8048`.
  * This confirms the observation from Stage A.1: on this specific out-of-domain stock footage cohort (Pexels), VideoMAE exhibits a strong bias toward predicting manipulation.
* **Known Manipulated Samples ($n=10$):**
  * VideoMAE selected the `fake` class for 9 of 10 known-manipulated samples.
  * Predicted fake probabilities ranged from `0.4801` (`vs4.mp4`, classified as `real`) to `0.9658` (`vs10.mp4`), with a mean fake score of `0.8354`.
* **Out-of-Domain Generalization Observation:**
  * While VideoMAE demonstrated high sensitivity to Remaker AI face swaps (90% identified), its inability to correctly classify authentic human footage in this cohort demonstrates domain specificity to FaceForensics++ C23 and precludes reliance on VideoMAE as an autonomous standalone authority.

### Forensic Interpretation
RealForensics and VideoMAE clearly rely on orthogonal representations:
* **VideoMAE** processes broad spatiotemporal context across the whole video frame using 3D tubelet attention, capturing macro-temporal artifacts but suffering false positives on clean stock video.
* **RealForensics** focuses exclusively on landmark-aligned facial mouth/lower-face patches using temporal convolutional networks, capturing lip and facial motion inconsistencies while remaining invariant to background texture.

## 13. Strict Forensic Methodological Limitations & Boundaries

1. **Out-of-Domain Evaluation:** SDFVD (Pexels / Remaker AI) is an out-of-domain evaluation relative to FaceForensics++ C23 for both models. Performance here does not represent in-domain FF++ accuracy or in-the-wild forensic reliability.
2. **No Calibrated Probability:** Neither model's raw score can be interpreted as a true Bayesian probability or forensic confidence score.
3. **Sample Size:** While 26 samples (including 6 paired transformation derivatives) provides engineering characterization, it is not a statistical benchmark.
4. **Non-Equivalence of Agreement with Authenticity:** When both models agree (`both_agree_with_known_category`), it demonstrates concordant model representation on that sample, not absolute proof of real-world authenticity or fabrication.

## 14. Engineering Recommendations for Potential Stage A.3 Multi-Detector Integration

Based on empirical evidence from Stage A.2.1, A.2.2, and A.2.3:

1. **Keep Signals Distinct in API Schemas:** Do **not** fuse VideoMAE and RealForensics into a single aggregate confidence score. The API response schema should report both signals as independent indicators:
   ```json
   {
     "video_ai_detection": {
       "videomae": { "fake_score": 0.7482, "selected_class": "fake" },
       "realforensics": { "uncalibrated_logit": -4.1347, "selected_class": "human" },
       "signal_correlation": "models_disagree"
     }
   }
   ```
2. **Decision Framework Rule: Require Corroboration:** Because models disagreed on 35% of primary samples, neither detector alone should be allowed to drive high-severity forensic escalations without corroborating forensic signals (such as metadata anomalies, audio-video desynchronization, or container inconsistencies).
3. **Maintain Conservative Preprocessing Guards:** If 2D-FAN fails to detect facial landmarks or if video duration is under 25 frames, RealForensics must safely output `status="not_available"` rather than falling back to unaligned approximations.
